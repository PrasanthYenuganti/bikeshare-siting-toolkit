Where the Vermelhinhas could grow next: a station plan for Maricá built on Bublr’s playbook

Prasanth Yenuganti, Bublr Bikes (Milwaukee, USA) · for Victor Sales · 17 November 2026 · Independent planning sketch from open data, not an EPT plan

This plan applies the rules we use to grow Bublr in Milwaukee to Maricá’s open data: a 250 m hexagon grid scored on Victor’s criteria, Census 2022 population by district, OpenStreetMap streets, services and bus stops, and terrain. It proposes 52 new adult stations in three phases and compares them with three alternative strategies.

01 · THE PLAN

Three phases, densest gaps first

Phase 1 closes the biggest gaps right next to today’s network; on its own it reaches or passes EPT’s goal of 70 adult stations. Phase 2 fills in the four districts and opens a first cluster in Ponta Negra. Phase 3 completes the grid at Bublr-style spacing. Within each phase, stations are shared among the districts in proportion to the residents who live more than 500 m from a station today.

04 · STRATEGIES

Three ways to place the next 20 stations

Victor’s question is how to weigh coverage gaps against transit connections. To make the trade-off concrete, I placed 20 stations three ways under the same spacing rules and measured what each one buys. Switch each strategy on in the map’s “Compare a strategy” control.

StrategyHow sites are chosenBus stops nearbyBairros reached
Reading the trade-off. Densifying the core (A) brings the most residents within a short 300 m walk but reaches few neighbourhoods. Coverage first (C) reaches the most people within 500 m, but its stations are far apart and lean on few bus stops. Transit first (B) sits between them. The recommended plan blends all three: it scores sites on Victor’s six criteria and then requires each new station to connect to the network.
05 · METHOD

How the sites were chosen

Each hexagon gets a 0–1 rank on six criteria. The weights are the pairwise judgments I would give in Victor’s Essential assessment. Census income by small area was not available here, so the “distance to an existing station” gap stands in for the income criterion; Victor’s own income layer should replace it.

CriterionMeasured here asWeight
  • Spacing 450 mNo two new stations closer than 450 m, and none within 350 m of an existing one. Bublr uses about 300 m in its core and 600 m in neighbourhoods.
  • Never alonePhase 1–2 sites must be within 1.5 km of an existing or earlier station, about a five-minute ride. Ponta Negra’s new areas start as a cluster of several stations at once.
  • Fair share by districtStations are split among the four districts by residents living beyond 500 m of a station today.
  • Hills cost scoreTerrain over 5% cuts a site’s score by 15%, over 10% by 40%. Steep sites that still rank are good candidates for e-bikes.
  • Catchment floorA site needs at least 1,000 residents within 600 m.
  • Field check lastEach site is a 250 m hexagon, not an address. EPT staff should pick the exact corner: a bus stop, a school gate or a market, with sidewalk room for 12–16 docks.

07 · OPERATING IT

What Milwaukee’s experience adds

  • Fleet and docks. At EPT’s standard of 10 bikes per station, the plan needs about 520 more bikes. Plan 12–16 docks per station so returns rarely fail; Bublr sizes busy stations above their bike count for the same reason.
  • Movable docks for Phase 3. Bublr’s newer battery-powered docks can be moved. Piloting outer sites on movable equipment lets EPT correct a weak placement after one season.
  • Rebalancing follows density. Clusters are cheaper to rebalance than scattered stations. Itaipuaçu’s long beachfront line will need a scheduled loop.
  • E-bikes for slopes. Parts of Inoã, Flamengo and the edges of Itaipuaçu climb toward the hills; the map’s slope layer shows where assisted bikes would help most.
  • Measure after six months. EPT’s app already records every trip. Compare each new station’s trips per day with its neighbours and move the weakest.
  • Expected use. Maricá recorded 202,963 trips in 2023 from 25 stations. Even at 1,500–3,000 trips per new station per year, well below that early average, the 52 stations would add about 80,000–155,000 trips a year.
  • Link to Tarifa Zero. Of the 52 proposed sites, 34 have at least one mapped bus stop within 400 m. EPT’s full stop and timetable data (GTFS) would sharpen the transit criterion.
  • Name stations for places. Riders find “Escola Municipal X” faster than “Estação 74”.
08 · LIMITS AND SOURCES

What to check before using this

This is a desk exercise from open data. Station positions are digitised (±110 m) and the network has grown since the study map was made. Population inside districts follows mapped buildings and streets, so areas with incomplete mapping are under-counted. OpenStreetMap lists only 202 bus stops in Maricá, far fewer than EPT’s network, so the transit criterion is conservative. Elevation comes from a 90 m terrain model. Proposed sites are hexagons for field review, not final addresses.

09 · RESUMO

Resumo em português

Este exercício aplica a Maricá as regras que usamos para expandir a Bublr em Milwaukee: espaçamento de cerca de 450 m, estações sempre conectadas à rede existente, distribuição justa entre os distritos e penalidade para terrenos íngremes. Com dados abertos (Censo 2022, OpenStreetMap e relevo), propomos 52 novas estações adultas em três fases (12, 20 e 20).

A Fase 1 sozinha leva a rede à meta da EPT de 70 estações adultas. Ao final, a parcela de moradores a até 500 m de uma estação passaria de cerca de 34% para 63%, com os maiores ganhos em Inoã, Itaipuaçu e Ponta Negra. As localizações são hexágonos de 250 m para verificação em campo pela EPT, não endereços finais.
